Why Did LXDE Switch to LXQt?

The transition from LXDE to LXQt was primarily driven by the deprecation of the GTK+ 2 toolkit, technical dissatisfaction with GTK+ 3, and the successful experimentation with the Qt framework. Facing mounting maintenance burdens and performance regressions with modern GTK libraries, the LXDE developers decided to rewrite the desktop environment using Qt, eventually merging their efforts with the Razor-qt project to form LXQt.

The Limitations of GTK+ 3

LXDE (Lightweight X11 Desktop Environment) was originally built using GTK+ 2, a mature and lightweight toolkit that fit the project’s goal of delivering a low-resource desktop. However, when the GNOME project moved development focus to GTK+ 3, GTK+ 2 reached its end-of-life cycle, forcing the LXDE team to look toward the future.

When lead developer Hong Jen Yee (PCMan) attempted to port key components like the PCManFM file manager to GTK+ 3, the results were unsatisfactory. GTK+ 3 introduced significant memory overhead, broke backward compatibility frequently across minor releases, and pushed design paradigms—such as client-side decorations—that clashed with LXDE's traditional, minimalist layout. Upgrading to GTK+ 3 fundamentally compromised the lightweight nature of the environment.

The Qt Experiment

In early 2013, Hong Jen Yee experimented with porting PCManFM to Qt to see if it could serve as a viable alternative. Contrary to the common perception that Qt was inherently bloated compared to GTK, the Qt port demonstrated memory usage that was comparable to GTK+ 2 and significantly lower than GTK+ 3.

In addition to superior resource management, the developers found the Qt framework to be technically superior for their needs. Qt offered a mature object-oriented architecture, better cross-platform support, consistent APIs, and well-maintained documentation, making ongoing maintenance far simpler than continuing with GTK.

The Merger with Razor-qt

While the LXDE team was porting their components to Qt, another independent open-source project called Razor-qt was pursuing the exact same goal: building a lightweight desktop environment powered by Qt. Recognizing that their goals, philosophies, and target audiences completely overlapped, the two development teams decided to collaborate rather than compete.

In July 2013, the LXDE and Razor-qt teams announced their merger. The combined developers retired Razor-qt and the GTK-based LXDE codebase to focus their joint efforts on building LXQt, ensuring a sustainable, modern, and lightweight desktop environment for low-spec hardware and efficiency-focused users.
