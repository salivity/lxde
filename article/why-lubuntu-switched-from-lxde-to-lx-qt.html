Why Lubuntu Switched from LXDE to LXQt

Lubuntu officially transitioned from the Lightweight X11 Desktop Environment (LXDE) to the Lightweight Qt Desktop Environment (LXQt) in October 2018 with the release of Lubuntu 18.10. This fundamental shift was driven by the obsolescence of the aging GTK 2 toolkit upon which LXDE was built, the upstream development stagnation of LXDE, and the rise of LXQt as an active, modern, and modular successor capable of delivering high performance on modern hardware.

The Timeline of the Transition

Lubuntu used LXDE as its default desktop environment from its inception in 2009 through early 2018. The transition was planned years in advance, but the development team waited to ensure stability.

  • Lubuntu 18.04 LTS (April 2018): Served as the final version to ship with LXDE, providing long-term support for users who relied on the legacy desktop.
  • Lubuntu 18.10 (October 2018): Marked the official debut of LXQt as the default desktop, establishing a new baseline for all subsequent releases.

The Deprecation of GTK 2

The core catalyst for the change was the underlying graphical toolkit. LXDE was built using GTK 2. By the mid-2010s, the GNOME project had shifted entirely to GTK 3, and GTK 2 was nearing end-of-life status. Maintaining a distribution reliant on an unmaintained toolkit posed severe security vulnerabilities, lack of hardware acceleration, and integration issues with modern display servers like Wayland.

Upstream developers explored migrating LXDE to GTK 3. However, experiments revealed that GTK 3 significantly increased memory consumption and system overhead, defeating the purpose of a lightweight desktop environment.

The Emergence of LXQt

In 2013, Hong Jen Yee (known as PCMan), the primary developer of LXDE, experimented with porting core components like the PCManFM file manager to the Qt framework. He found that Qt 5 was significantly lighter, faster, and more modular than GTK 3, while offering cleaner development APIs.

Around the same time, the developers of Razor-qt—another lightweight Qt-based desktop project—faced similar goals. The LXDE and Razor-qt teams decided to merge their efforts, forming the LXQt project.

Why the Lubuntu Team Made the Move

  1. Active Upstream Maintenance: Upstream LXDE development had effectively ceased as developers shifted focus to LXQt. Staying on LXDE would have meant maintaining an abandoned codebase.
  2. Modern Technology Stack: LXQt is built on Qt, which receives active updates, robust tooling, and native support for modern features, including HiDPI displays and modern graphics architectures.
  3. Resource Efficiency: LXQt matched the core ethos of Lubuntu. While memory usage was marginally higher than GTK 2, it was substantially lower and more responsive than GTK 3 or GTK 4 equivalents.
  4. Shift in Lubuntu's Mission: Alongside the switch to LXQt, the Lubuntu project updated its mission statement. Rather than focusing exclusively on reviving ancient computers, the distribution pivoted toward providing a fast, modern, and uncluttered operating system that gives users maximum control over system resources on contemporary hardware.
